1. Daily Bidding System

Two Daily Windows (Nepal Time)

  • 10:00–11:00 AM: Review and bid on fresh Australian listings.
  • 4:00–5:00 PM: Second sweep for new listings and active replies.
  • Last 10 minutes: Log bids, respond to messages, and flag follow-ups.

Target Volume

Aim for 4–8 high-fit bids per day, not 20 copy-paste bids. Personalisation and response speed matter more than volume.

Bid Priority Score Bid only if ≥ 7/10

FactorScore
Posted within the last 2 hours0–2
Clear scope, deliverables, or examples0–2
Strong relevant sample available0–2
Commercially viable budget or project size0–2
Payment method, good history, or low competition0–2

Walk Away When

  • Several services are expected for the price of one.
  • The brief is unclear and the client will not clarify it.
  • Unlimited revisions, free work, or speculative concepts are demanded.
  • The required skill, licence, language, or turnaround cannot be delivered properly.
  • No relevant proof exists and the brief is high-risk or heavily competitive.

A bad-fit job is not a lead. It is a distraction wearing a budget label.

2. Proposal Framework (6-Part Bid)

1. Personalised Hook1–2 sentences

Reference one concrete detail: platform, goal, deliverable, audience, issue, or deadline.

2. Outcome Statement1 sentence

State what the client gets, not the tools being used.

3. Relevant Proof1–2 sentences

Use one specific approved sample, outcome, client type, or capability.

4. Scope3 concise bullets

Mirror the brief and include only what is actually included.

5. One Practical Question1 question

Ask the single question that most affects scope, timing, or quality.

6. Clear CTANext action

Say what you need next and when work can begin.

Base Structure 120–170 words

Hi [Client name],

I noticed you need [specific job detail]. I can help you achieve [clear outcome] by [brief approach].

For similar [project/client type], I have [specific proof or relevant sample].

For this project, I would deliver:

  • [Deliverable tied to the brief]
  • [Deliverable tied to the brief]
  • [Deliverable tied to the brief]

To scope this accurately: [one meaningful question]?

If you send [required input], I can confirm the approach, timeline, and next step today.

Thanks,
Sandip

Key Rules

  • Keep most bids between 120–170 words.
  • Start with the client’s problem, never a biography.
  • Use exactly three deliverables unless the job genuinely needs more.
  • Match the client’s tone.
  • Attach only a close-match portfolio sample.
  • Ask one question only.

3. Service Playbooks

A. Web Development & Website Fixes

  • Broken sites, speed, forms, mobile issues, launch deadlines
  • Shopify, WordPress, Webflow, or Squarespace
  • Conversion-focused pages and clean handover

B. Graphic Design & Branding

  • Brand consistency across existing assets
  • Specific channel, campaign, print format, or audience
  • Source files and production-ready delivery

C. Video Editing & AI Content

  • Retention, pacing, story, subtitles, and platform formatting
  • Consistent characters and visuals across AI scenes
  • Approved pilot before full production

D. Social Media & Meta Ads

  • Content tied to business outcomes
  • Reels, captions, calendars, community, and reporting
  • Lead generation, tracking, testing, and optimisation

E. SEO

  • Technical and on-page review
  • Keyword and competitor direction
  • Prioritised fixes and realistic reporting

For every category: understand the client’s goal, guard the scope, deliver quality, and ask for a testimonial.

4. Scoping & Scope Guardrails

Define Clearly Upfront

Pages, platform, revisions, content responsibility, integrations, formats, assets, and timelines.

What’s Included

List exactly what is in scope. Three concise bullets are usually enough.

What’s Not Included

Content writing, stock images, hosting, domain, third-party costs, or ongoing maintenance unless agreed.

Revision Policy

Define what counts as a revision and put major direction changes outside scope.

Client Responsibilities

Assets, access, logins, content, approvals, and feedback deadlines.

5. Follow-Up System

One Value-Adding Follow-Up

Use one follow-up only when the task is still active:

Hi [Client name], just checking whether you still need support with [specific outcome]. I can share a relevant example or clarify the proposed scope if that helps you decide.
  • Do not chase repeatedly.
  • Do not discount immediately.
  • Do not offer unpaid custom work.

Never spam. Be helpful, short, and human.

6. Quality & Trust Signals

  • Relevant portfolio samples only
  • Clear communication and fast replies
  • Professional scope and proposal structure
  • Delivery on time with agreed expectations
  • Testimonial and reusable case study after every win

7. Bid Tracker & Two-Week Review

Track Every Bid

Date, category, job title/link, posted age, bid time, amount, proposal version, proof attached, client reply, shortlist, assignment, loss reason, and notes.

Metrics After 14 Days

  • Total bids and bids sent within 2 hours
  • Reply, shortlist, and assignment rates
  • Average bid value
  • Best category, hook, and proof asset
  • Common loss reason

Review Decisions

  • Double down on the two categories with the best reply and assignment rates.
  • Rewrite hooks that get no replies after 10 relevant bids.
  • Remove proof that does not create conversations.
  • Raise or narrow pricing when weak jobs consume attention.
  • Turn every win into a case study.

8. Pre-Send Checklist

Final Standard

A winning Airtasker proposal is not a biography. It is a small, credible plan for this client’s job. Be specific, honest, fast, and guard the scope.

How to Use It

Review before both bidding sessions, follow the flow for every 7/10+ lead, track results, and review performance every 14 days.

Operating Rule

Bid fast on the right jobs, prove relevance, set scope early, and learn from every result.
